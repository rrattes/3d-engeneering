# Optional product style vocabulary

Choose a direction from the brief. These five styles are starting points, not fixed
presets or required options. Their examples describe tendencies, not permissions to
change interfaces, performance or manufacture. Read only the selected sections.

Engineering requirements constrain every style below. Reject or redesign a styling
choice that worsens function, safety, performance, maintenance or manufacture.
An unresolved relevant technical impact prevents accepting that styling proposal
as the product solution. Style-specific suggestions do not override this rule.

## Minimalista

- Character: calm, precise, visually simple.
- Form: legible main volume; controlled proportion changes; consistent transitions.
- Construction: organized seams; aligned controls; deliberate hierarchy between body and base.
- CMF: restrained palette; an accent only where it communicates interaction or identity.
- Review: inspect whether the product has a recognizable silhouette and intentional details at use distance. A rounded generic enclosure alone does not establish a design direction.
- Tradeoff: hidden fixings or continuous surfaces must retain maintenance access. Large FDM faces need a credible orientation/finish proposal; renders should not erase layer texture when evaluating print appearance.

## Gamer

- Character: energetic, technical or performance-oriented, with the intensity chosen for the audience.
- Form: directional massing or controlled facets; one dominant visual gesture rather than unrelated cuts.
- Construction: ventilation, controls and panels share alignment and a common geometric language. Lighting is optional and requires a feasible hardware proposal if included.
- CMF: a dominant surface and limited contrast; use accent placement to reinforce hierarchy.
- Review: silhouette, feature hierarchy and openings should remain coherent with accent lighting switched off. Explain how the form fits the product context.
- Tradeoff: ornamental vents cannot replace airflow design. Sharp fins, obstructed ports and unmanufacturable recesses require redesign.

## Industrial

- Character: robust, serviceable and functionally legible; intensity can range from precise equipment to rugged tooling.
- Form: volumes follow components and handling; protect vulnerable edges where the use requires it.
- Construction: organized visible fixings, repeated spacing, clear removable panels and readable interaction points.
- CMF: honest material/process expression; distinguish working surfaces and touchpoints when useful.
- Review: exposed mechanisms and details should communicate purpose. Avoid adding bolts, ribs or hazards merely to imply strength.
- Tradeoff: exposed fixings must meet corrosion, cleaning and contact constraints. Visual robustness is not evidence of load capacity.

## Orgânico

- Character: fluid, approachable or inspired by natural growth.
- Form: flowing silhouette, gradual section changes and intentional curvature; assess continuity in the chosen CAD representation.
- Construction: place seams where they support flow and assembly; relate grips/contact surfaces to use.
- CMF: use texture and contrast to support the form without implying an unavailable process.
- Review: inspect front/side/top silhouettes and highlights; identify abrupt transitions or swollen volumes that obscure function. A soft render does not prove smooth CAD surfaces.
- Tradeoff: double curvature can increase supports, size and cleaning difficulty. Claims of comfort require physical evaluation; visible roundness alone is insufficient.

## Retrô

- Character: deliberately reference a chosen era or product family; state the reference period before borrowing details.
- Form: proportions, softened corners or planar bevels derived from the chosen reference language, not random nostalgia.
- Construction: panels, controls, louvers and markings form a coherent vocabulary with modern access needs.
- CMF: period-inspired color and texture adapted to the actual material and environment.
- Review: check consistency across silhouette, controls and finish. Modern connectors may be integrated through deliberate panel hierarchy.
- Tradeoff: a decorative louver still needs engineering clearance; period styling must preserve current functional requirements. Reference provenance and requested branding remain explicit.

## Custom and mixed directions

Capture the same decisions as the five entries: character, silhouette, surfaces,
construction, CMF and observable criteria. For a mixture, select a dominant grammar
and specific borrowed traits, for example calm primary volume with one directional
technical accent. Compare conflicts in transitions, seam layout and color hierarchy.
Do not mechanically merge every trait from both styles.

Style choice never determines physical dimensions, hardware, material or printer
without the project's own requirements. Use visual references supplied by the user;
if outside references are sought, retrieve them with provenance and distinguish
reference images from the product being developed.

## Convert style to reviewable decisions

For the selected style, name the actual part/feature and one observable criterion.
Use the product's own dimensions and manufacturing constraints rather than a fixed
style recipe. Examples below are review prompts, not universal acceptance values.

| Direction | Concrete design decision | Manufacturing check | Review evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimalista | Align removable panel seams with the main volume hierarchy | Tool access and visible-face orientation | Overall view plus seam detail |
| Gamer | Give exterior panels one directional facet language | Supported bevels; protected inlet sections unchanged | Opaque silhouette with lighting off |
| Industrial | Make service boundaries and fastener rhythm legible | Corrosion/contact limits; assembly sequence | Service view and fastener detail |
| Orgânico | Connect selected surfaces with intentional curvature | Support removal, wall thickness, cleaning access | Orthographic silhouettes and highlight view |
| Retrô | Apply one era's proportion/louver vocabulary consistently | Real vent clearance and contemporary connectors | Main proportion view and connector panel |

Do not mark the style successful merely because colors match. For a CMF-only task,
review only the requested color/material/finish criteria and retain the existing form.

For substyles and reference cards, consult [concept_productivity.md](concept_productivity.md)
only when a project needs deeper direction. Machine-readable vocabularies are optional,
not universal style definitions or proof of aesthetic success.
