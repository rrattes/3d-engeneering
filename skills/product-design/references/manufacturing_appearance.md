# Appearance that can be made

Assess actual proposed material, process, machine, orientation, assembly and finish.
Keep unknowns as assumptions. Rendering plastic like anodized metal does not make
it metal; smooth shading can conceal facets or layer effects.

For FDM, relate visible faces and seams to orientation, layer texture, supports,
bridges, edge quality, warping and access for finishing. Thin ribs, recess walls and
insert regions need geometric/manufacturing checks before aesthetic acceptance.
Do not promise support-free printing from a clean render. Individual part galleries
usually auto-scale and do not establish printer compatibility.

For other processes, identify the relevant constraints instead of importing FDM
rules. Material changes may affect stiffness, temperature, wear, chemicals and
weathering; finish/color can matter in the operating environment. A physical material
change is not merely a visualization-only CMF study. Route those effects to engineering.

Record desired appearance, proposed production route, evidence already available,
sample required and remaining judgment. Physical texture, gloss, real color and
handling comfort require appropriate samples/user evaluation. Prototypes should
resolve the highest-risk interface or finish first, rather than spending on the
whole cosmetic shell before its construction is viable.
